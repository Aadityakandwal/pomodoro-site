        The Pomodoro Technique

        Born in the late 1980s from Francesco Cirillo's kitchen timer (shaped like a tomato — pomodoro in Italian), this technique turns sprawling work into rhythmic, manageable pulses.

        • Intervals — 25 minutes of focused work, no interruptions, one task at a time.
        • Short breaks — 5 minutes to stretch, hydrate, reset attention.
        • Long breaks — After four pomodoros, 15–30 minutes of genuine rest.
        • Flow — The rhythm itself becomes a cue for deep work.

        What makes this timer different

        • Layered ambient mixer — combine rain, campfire, coffee shop, and noise layers.
        • Web Worker timer — runs in a dedicated thread with absolute-time math, so backgrounded tabs never drift.
        • Local-first music — your MP3s stay on your device. No upload, no account.
        • Mini player & ghost mode — the interface disappears when you don't need it.

        Frequently asked questions

        What is the Pomodoro Technique?

        A time-management method that breaks work into 25-minute focused intervals separated by short breaks, with a longer break after every four pomodoros.

        Can I use custom sounds while studying?

        Yes. A layered ambient mixer with independent volume controls, plus your own MP3s (stored locally), SoundCloud links, or the free Internet Archive.

        Does the timer run in the background?

        Yes. It runs inside a dedicated Web Worker that uses absolute wall-clock timestamps instead of interval counting, so it keeps accurate time even when the browser throttles background tabs.
